# Know Your Weingarten Rights

> **For union members.** A one-page guide to your right to union representation
> in an investigatory meeting. Operational guidance, not legal advice — public-
> sector and state rules vary; check with your steward or labor rep.

## What Weingarten rights are

If you are called into a meeting with management that you **reasonably believe
could lead to discipline**, you have the right to have a union representative
present. This comes from a 1975 U.S. Supreme Court case, *NLRB v. J. Weingarten,
Inc.*

## When they apply

- The meeting is **investigatory** — management is seeking facts or your answers.
- You **reasonably believe** it could result in discipline (a warning,
  suspension, discharge, or a mark on your record).
- Both must be true. A meeting just to *inform* you of a decision already made,
  or to give routine instructions, generally is **not** covered.

## You must ask — it isn't automatic

In most cases the employer does **not** have to tell you about this right, and
they do **not** have to stop unless you **request** representation. So ask.
Memorize or carry this line:

> **"If this discussion could in any way lead to my being disciplined or
> terminated, or affect my personal working conditions, I respectfully request
> that my union representative be present. Without representation, I choose not
> to answer any questions."**

## What happens after you ask

Once you request representation, the employer must choose one of three options:

1. **Grant it** — stop and wait for your rep, or
2. **End the interview**, or
3. **Offer you the choice** of continuing without a rep or ending the meeting.

The employer may **not** simply deny your request and keep questioning you, and
may **not** retaliate against you for asking.

## What your rep can (and can't) do

- **Can:** ask for the subject of the meeting beforehand, take you aside to
  confer, advise you, ask clarifying questions, and object to abusive
  questioning.
- **Can't:** answer for you or turn it into a bargaining session. It is still
  management's meeting.

## If your rights are denied

- Stay calm and professional. **Do not** walk out without saying so, and **do
  not** be insubordinate — comply under protest if ordered.
- State clearly: *"I am answering under protest because my request for
  representation was denied."*
- Write down what happened as soon as possible: who, what was said, when.
- Tell your steward immediately — a denial may itself be an unfair labor
  practice.

## Quick reminders

- ☐ Stay calm; be respectful.
- ☐ Ask for representation **out loud**, using the statement above.
- ☐ Don't answer questions until your rep arrives.
- ☐ Tell the truth; never lie or destroy anything.
- ☐ Write down what happened afterward.

---

_Public-sector employees: similar rights often exist under state law, but the
details differ. Verify with your labor representative. Not legal advice._
