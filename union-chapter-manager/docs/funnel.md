# The combined funnel: course + SaaS

How the course and the Union Chapter Manager app feed each other. The short
version: **the content builds trust and an audience; the software turns that
audience into recurring revenue.** Neither works nearly as well alone.

## The flow

```
   Free lead magnet            Paid course              SaaS subscription
  ┌──────────────────┐      ┌────────────────┐       ┌────────────────────┐
  │ "Grievance intake │ ──▶ │ How to Run Your │ ──▶  │ Union Chapter      │
  │  checklist" (PDF) │      │ Union Local     │       │ Manager (monthly/  │
  │  + email capture  │      │ $149 one-time   │       │ annual)            │
  └──────────────────┘      └────────────────┘       └────────────────────┘
        builds the list         earns + qualifies         recurring revenue
                                                            (the real prize)
        ▲                                                          │
        └───────────  in-app "Learn" tab teases the course  ◀──────┘
```

Two entry points, one loop:
1. **Content-first:** a free checklist → email list → course → app.
2. **Product-first:** someone lands in the app, opens the **Learn** tab, and the
   free lessons upsell the course. The app itself is a funnel for the course,
   and the course is a funnel for the app.

## Why each stage exists

| Stage | Job | Why it matters |
|-------|-----|----------------|
| **Lead magnet** (free) | Capture email in exchange for one genuinely useful template | You own the audience. This is the asset that makes everything else profitable. |
| **Course** ($149, one-time) | Monetize trust, teach the workflow | Qualifies serious buyers and pays for your acquisition. High margin. |
| **SaaS** (recurring) | Do the work the course teaches | The durable revenue. One customer pays every month instead of once. |
| **In-app Learn tab** | Re-sell the course to active users | Free distribution to your warmest audience — people already using the tool. |

## Rough conversion math (be skeptical, these are planning numbers)

Assume you build an email list from the lead magnet. Typical cold-ish funnel
rates:

- Lead magnet landing page → email signup: **~30%**
- Email subscriber → course buyer (over time, with nurture): **~2%**
- Course buyer → app subscriber: **~15–30%** (warm, already bought in)

**Worked example — 5,000 people reach the lead magnet page in year one:**

| Step | Rate | Result |
|------|------|--------|
| Land on lead-magnet page | — | 5,000 |
| Join email list | 30% | 1,500 |
| Buy the course @ $149 | 2% of list | 30 buyers → **$4,470** |
| Subscribe to app @ ~$49/mo | 25% of buyers | ~8 chapters → **~$4,700/yr recurring, growing** |

Read the lesson in those numbers honestly:
- The **course alone** is a few thousand dollars — a nice validation, not a
  living. (This matches the "courses are high-margin, low-median-revenue"
  reality.)
- The **recurring app revenue compounds**: year-two subscribers stack on year
  one. That's where this becomes a real business.
- **Everything scales with the top of the funnel.** 5k visitors is modest; the
  entire model is gated by how many of the right people you get in front of.
  That's why the free content and list-building are the actual work.

## Sequencing (what to do first)

1. **Ship the lead magnet + one template.** Cheapest thing that starts building
   the list. Do this before anything else.
2. **Release the $49 playbook / mini-course** (course modules 1–3 + templates).
   Validates that people pay you for this knowledge.
3. **Turn on the app for early buyers** — hand-hold your first chapters, learn
   what they need, get testimonials.
4. **Expand to the full $149 course** and the course-in-app bundle.
5. **Add billing** to the app (Stripe) once you have paying chapters asking to
   pay you.

## Pricing the bundle

- Course: **$149 one-time.**
- App: **~$29–79/chapter/month** (chapters have small budgets but real ones;
  price per chapter, not per user — officers change constantly).
- **Bundle:** course included free with an **annual** app plan. Makes the annual
  commitment feel like a discount and pulls one-time buyers into recurring.

## What NOT to do

- Don't build both at full scale simultaneously. Lead with the content wedge.
- Don't build course hosting into the app — link out to a course platform.
- Don't over-produce the course v1. A finished mini-course beats a perfect one
  that never ships.
